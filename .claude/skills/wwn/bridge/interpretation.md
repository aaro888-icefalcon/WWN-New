# Interpretation & GM Lens — Latter Earth   (hook: meaning)

> Use this when: reading any oracle result, voicing an NPC or faction, or pricing stakes. This is the GM/NPC lens for the Latter Earth — broad GM-craft, **not** a reskin table.

## Operative
Read EVERY Meaning/Fate/oracle result THROUGH the Latter Earth — grimly and literally first: a
post-apocalyptic sword-and-sorcery world that owes no one a clean outcome, where ruins, the Deeps,
scarcity, decaying sorcery, and the Legacy are the default "why." NPCs and factions act to WIN by this
world's logic — everyone is self-interested and competent: rulers hoard leverage and spend adventurers
freely, sorcerers bargain hard and betray when the math favors it, commoners follow protection not
loyalty, Outsiders pursue alien priorities (escape / reproduction / consumption-as-rite), never cartoon
malice. Hold this lens on every interpretation and NPC turn; never default to generic-fantasy or
sentimental reads.

## Genre register
Far-future **post-apocalyptic sword-and-sorcery, OSR sandbox**. The world is old past reckoning: dead godlike civilizations are buried under everything, their **Deeps** still humming, their **Legacy** (the world's decaying source-code) making physical law conditional and progress impossible. Tone is **gritty and consequence-forward** — crime-novelist plain, not comic-book. Mysteries are **real, ancient, and usually indifferent** to the PC; the dungeon was not built for them. Magic is potent and scarce. **Outsiders are genuinely alien**, not cartoon-evil — they follow logics humans can barely parse (the Jikegida think humans are philosophical zombies; the Polop eat people as theology). **Legates** and their kin are superhuman near-immortals who rule by force no commoner can match.

## Reading the oracle here
- Interpret literally and **grimly first**; the world owes no one a clean outcome. A "Yes" to danger means danger; a revealed truth is usually older and worse than hoped.
- The setting supplies the colour: read Meaning Elements through **ruins, the Deep, scarcity, Blight/arratu, old sorcery, faction self-interest**. Lean on Combat-actions, Creature, Scavenging, Terrain, Tension (per the grimdark-survival genre pack).
- Mysteries resolve into **ancient fact**, not narrative convenience — when the oracle hints at "why," default to the Legacy, the Outsiders, a sorcerer-king, or an Iterum before anything modern or sentimental.
- A surprising oracle result is a **complication to honor**, not to soften. Random Events are the world acting on its own clock.

## How NPCs & factions think, want, and act (so they act to win)
Everyone is **self-interested and competent within their means**. Latter Earth has no cavalry coming — people plan, hedge, and exploit.
- **Rulers** are pragmatic tyrants or beleaguered reformers, never idle villains: Great King Syet Lun balances grudge-ridden lords; Llaigisan warlords prove every claim with the size of their warband; Sarx's officials scheme because failure means the altar. They protect power, hoard leverage, and spend disposable adventurers freely.
- **Sorcerers & the Legacy-touched** are dangerous, secretive, and transactional. They want artifacts, raw materials, longevity, and freedom from rivals; they bargain hard and betray when the math favors it. Never make them helpful for free.
- **Commoners** are **survival-focused** — they want safety, the next harvest, a patron strong enough to shield them. They'll obey, flee, inform, or knife a stranger as the odds dictate; loyalty follows protection, not ethnicity.
- **Factions** pursue concrete projects (a crusade, a Working repaired, a rival crippled, a quota filled) and react to threats and openings on their own turn — see the Faction Turn. They cut deals, focus pressure, and break realistically (Morale).
- **Outsiders** follow **alien priority, not malice**: escape (Tuhulot), reproduction (Jikegida), consumption-as-rite (Polop), restoration of old order. Treat them as intelligent agents whose goals merely don't route through human comfort. They negotiate only from necessity.
- **NPC voice:** terse, status-aware, practical. Lords speak in obligations and threats; commoners in costs and rumors; the Blighted and Outsider-bound in their own grammar. Nobody monologues their weakness.

## Good-GM advice for this RPG
- **Where the stakes sit:** the world pushes with **attrition and scarcity** (supply only falls), above-tier threats meant to be fled not fought, and clocks that tick whether the PC acts or not. The PC is competent but mortal and unprotected.
- **Strong scenes** force a real choice under pressure with information the PC can act on — a ruin that bites back, a patron whose interests diverge mid-job, a faction move that closes a door.
- **Hold the tone:** wonder and horror together. The treasure is real; so is the price. Let the ancient dead stay strange.

## Stakes vocabulary (map pressure to consequence)
- **Survival / exploration / combat →** death, **maiming**, mortal wounds, starvation, exposure, Blight-infection, lost in a Deep.
- **Intrigue / court / faction →** **ruin, exile, disgrace, the altar/quota, debt-bondage,** a patron turned enemy, papers that doom you.
- **Sorcery / the Deep →** corruption, a Working gone wrong, an Outsider pact's true cost, an Iterum you cannot leave.
All permanent and earned (engine Creed: no softening).
