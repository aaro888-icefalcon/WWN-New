# Setting Canon — Latter Earth / Agathon / the Amundi Kingdoms   (ground truth; overrides recollection)

> **This is the LIVE canon this campaign starts from.** It re-anchors the default Latter Earth to **Agathon**, in **the Amundi Kingdoms**, starting in the plagued mining town of **Khatin** in the Despotate of **Mishar**. Entries here outrank model memory. Fate-Question answers about canon get written back here. Detail only the starting region; grow the rest on demand.

## Premise / tone
The **Latter Earth** is the unimaginably far future — a world so old that countless godlike empires lie buried beneath its dust, and the very laws of reality (the **Legacy**) are decaying. There is no progress, no science, no rescue: only the dangerous present and the lethal, treasure-laden past. Gritty post-apocalyptic sword-and-sorcery; adventurers are recognized, disposable, and on their own. (The whole world is one **Iterum** — echoed reality, coded *108 Triumphant Void* — so anything may be changed without "breaking canon.")

## The Latter Earth in brief
- **The Legacy** — the world's decaying source-code. Physical law is conditional and special-cased; magic and Workings fail unpredictably over ages. Why nothing advances, and why wildernesses a day from a capital teem with horrors no army can clear.
- **The Deeps** — vast buried megastructures dug by the Outsiders; the classic dungeon. New ones surface constantly; cities sit atop older buried ones.
- **Outsiders** — the aliens who once ruled and enslaved humanity from the Deeps; now hunted remnants, genuinely **alien** in motive. They trapped themselves here forever and hate humanity.
- **Arratu** — xenoformed wastelands left by the Outsiders; alien atmospheres, mutant flora/fauna, well-preserved ruins. Can spread on their own (e.g. the **Starkwald** east of Vois).
- **Sorcerer-kings, god-heroes & Legates** — superhuman, near-immortal figures whose heirs founded the nations. In Amund, the age of god-heroes is **burnt out**; only fading remnants and the rare new-born divinity remain.
- **The Blighted** — cursed human-descended peoples; the **Anakim** (Anak) are warborn, hate-driven Blighted of the wastes (here, the **Shinbu Anak** pirates to the east).

## AGATHON — the continent (high level; detail on demand at the frontier)
The great northern continent, ancient heartland of fallen empires. Powers of note (distant until play reaches them):
- **The Amundi Kingdoms** — the campaign's home; see below. Graveyard of gods between the **Carven Peaks** (west; the vanished Pale Emperor's undead crews still carve whole mountains) and the **Lakaian Range**.
- **Vitrum** — proud empire of glass and iron, heir to the Intoners' sonic sorcery; fights Sath Ingit, Hyperborea, and the Verdancy while its senate schemes for an oft-vacant throne.
- **Sath Ingit** — beast-riders whose beastwrights mold flesh (at costs to longevity and sanity); raid Vitrum.
- **The Verdancy** — a multicolored hell of lethal forest and flesh-and-flora hybrids; the demented remnant of a green-worshipping theocracy.
- **The Black Pact** — a loose confederacy of great arcanists in lonely towers; their evil sendings bleed across borders.
- **Couront** — cold pine-and-barley land of knightly houses and the Church of the Bleeding God; plagued by curses out of the Black Pact. (Exiles from Couront drift to Pelegrin's port of Voigne.)
- **The Aristoi Principalities / Shinbu Anak** — refined Anak nobility and their savage cousins; Shinbu pirates raid Ostmark, Kytheron, and the Herondeep.
- **Ninth Leng · Ondas · the Tribes of Hyperborea · the Still Cities** — priest-haunted cold, a westward reclaiming menaced by automatons from beneath the Pale, proud raiders, and miming undead, respectively.
- **Seas & barriers:** the **Gulf of Amund** (relatively safe, pirate-trafficked), the **Herondeep** (Shinbu piracy), the **Hammersea** (Fidach's port of Kennet), the **Carven Peaks** (W), the **Cut** & the cursed **Spoil** (S, severing Amund from Sarx/Nakad).
- **Across the water:** **Sarx**, the blood-altar empire of the Red Gods, raids Amund's coasts by sea for sacrifices. The fractured **Gyre** and the wider Atlas lie far off and indifferent (pointer: `book/The-Atlas-of-the-Latter-Earth/02-The-Lands-of-the-West/`).

## THE AMUNDI KINGDOMS — home region (god-burnt Agathon)
Raucous, ever-changing heartlands where dozens of god-kingdoms have left their bones. Once an incubator of god-heroes and demigods; that grace **burned out in 378 BL**, when the Thousand Gods died driving back the eldritch eidolons of Nakad's Gods of Truth, carving the **Cut** and leaving the cursed **Spoil** as their grave. Now seven domains and dozens of petty states feud for gold, glory, and rule. Slavery is common; law is lax but vengeance is sure; social class is stark yet fluid — a self-made hero (or a successful adventurer) can rise to a king's right hand, or take the axe. **Rulers constantly need trusted outside agents** — adventurers wholly reliant on royal protection, and convenient to blame.
- **Qasir** (Emirate) — southernmost, on the Spoil's edge; **Emir Seif** also heads the **Great Faith** of the Thousand Gods. Capital **Rukh**; Spoil-gateway towns **Yath** (lawless) & **Hazirim** (noble exile-prison).
- **Mishar** (Despotate) — **the starting domain.** Broke from Qasir a century ago; ruled by **Despot Amiya**, a bloodthirsty queen who delights in barbarous splendor, preys on Nabardura, and crushes her people with taxes from her seat at **Jilat** (mighty walls; a great arena — champions favored, losers fed to the dogs). People are **Qasiri** (lean, amber-hued, dark-haired, black-eyed).
- **Nabardura** (Satrapies) — quarrelsome near-independent satraps under Great King **Darius** at **Capavan** (city of abandoned palaces where "many things now dwell").
- **Pelegrin** (Kingdom) — decadent Seven Rivers heir; mighty, indolent King **Amalric**; pageantry-loving nobles, crushed serfs (the **Bleeding God** is strong among them). Capital **Rais Egrin**.
- **Vois** (Duchy) — anti-noble burghers under hard-pressed Duke **Clotaire**; the **Starkwald arratu** presses monsters on capital **Rais Vois**.
- **Ostmark** (Duchy) — Duke **Franz** holds back the Shinbu Anak (and is whispered to bargain with them); capital **Mundburgh**.
- **Fidach** (Clans) — leaderless forest clans; warriors hire south as mercenaries (mart: **Ganzabara**); port **Kennet** on the Hammersea. No slavery.
- **The Lost March** — a deliberate blank on every map; anything may be true there.
- **Faiths:** the **Great Faith** (Thousand Gods, of Qasir/Mishar), the **City Gods** (a city's massed heroes/divinities), the **Need Gods** (generic appeals brokered by priests), and the disturbing **Living Gods** — actual living deities, some divine beasts ravenous for sacrifice, born of summoning or Amund's old god-birthing tendency, fearsome within a single region.

## STARTING REGION — Khatin, a plagued mining town of Mishar (detailed)
Khatin is a hill-and-forest mining town in southern Mishar, its warriors holding back forest beasts from the farms below, its ore and charcoal feeding Despot Amiya's distant war-coffers. Above it climb the **Unzent works** — a played-out older mine, now disused and shunned. For three seasons a wasting sickness, **the grey shudder**, has gripped the town. *(Rolled tags: Community = Plagued City; Wilderness = Disused Mine; Hook = the grimoire in the battered laboratory.)*
- **The grey shudder (plague):** a creeping palsy and grey-filming of the skin that strikes **only the laboring class** — miners, charcoalers, serfs — never the grandees. Not very contagious; not always fatal; recurs in waves. Its true root lies up in the **Unzent works**, where an ancient **sorcerous laboratory** was breached.
- **The Unzent works** (disused mine / dungeon): an old strip-and-shaft mine that peeled back a buried megastructure, exposing a **battered magical laboratory** holding a **long-lost grimoire of power** — most likely the engine of the grey shudder. Within: a **crazed wildcat miner, Talorg**, squatting feral in the dark; an **ancient, still-friendly mining automaton**; rival crews **fighting over control of the mine**; a storehouse of refined ore; and a mound of centuries-old tailings. The Carven Peaks and their Pale-Emperor necromantic galleries loom further west.
- **The pull:** rich loot lies unplundered in the Unzent lab — and someone is hiring muscle to go in.

## Named NPCs (name — role — wants)
- **Xerxes** — merciless grandee and mine-lord of Khatin (Qasiri) — to profit from the grey shudder: he controls the only palliative and buys out the dying's claims for a song. *Acts:* work the sick to the last, deny the cure, blame outsiders.
- **Blasina** — traditional healer / herbwife of Khatin, wise in the plague's ways — to halt the cycle; she can only ease the shudder, not cure it, and suspects the Unzent works are its source. *Acts:* shelter the sick, seek the "key to halting the cycle," distrust grandees and sorcerers alike.
- **Mina** — a respected ascetic / hermit near the Unzent works; **secret identity** (Baneful Success) — to **curb a too-enthusiastic ally**. *Secret (PC-unknown):* she once was mistress of the little sorcerers' school and helped raise her protégé **Urcrin**; her past success now overreaches and threatens all. Unremarkably average; sharp, brisk, vigorous; paces constantly, prefers to stand.
- **Urcrin** — amoral sorcerer, "headmaster" of the small arcane school grown up by the old works — to seize the **long-lost grimoire of power** from the Unzent laboratory, heedless that delving it feeds the grey shudder. *Acts:* hire expendable muscle, smile and lie, spend others freely.
- **Talorg** — a crazed wildcat miner gone feral in the Unzent dark — survival, the mine as his alone. *Acts:* ambush, hoard, kill.
- **Despot Amiya** — bloodthirsty Queen of Mishar at Jilat (distant) — war, splendor, taxes, dominance over Nabardura. *Acts:* spend subjects freely; reward useful agents, discard failures.
- **Emir Seif** — lord of Qasir & head of the Great Faith (distant) — piety, the Thousand Gods' memory, recovery of Mishar.

## Key truths / tone
- No progress, no science, no cavalry; the world owes nothing and punishes passivity.
- Power is personal and brutal; rulers are pragmatic, sorcerers self-interested, commoners survival-bent, Outsiders alien. NPCs act to **win**.
- The past is real, ancient, mostly indifferent — and lethal to wake.
- Death, maiming, ruin, and exile are permanent (engine Creed). **Full lethality confirmed at Session Zero.**
